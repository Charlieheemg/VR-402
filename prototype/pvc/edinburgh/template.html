VR-405 / LOCAL ACOUSTIC EVIDENCE

Same voice. Same words. Different delivery.

Listen to one British English speaker reading matched TIMIT sentences. Compare plain speech with two instructed styles. Original MKH800 recordings; no playback normalization or processing.

Style labels ≠ intelligibility ground truth. These measurements describe the signal. This demonstration contains no human listener scores, intelligibility predictions or confidence scores.

What the measurements mean
  • File duration: samples ÷ sample rate, including leading/trailing silence. It is not articulation rate.
  • F0 range: openSMILE eGeMAPSv02 smoothed nonzero F0 80th–20th percentile range, in semitones. Pitch tracking is an estimate and has not been manually validated.
  • RMS level: whole-file digital amplitude in dBFS, including silence. Not calibrated sound pressure or perceived loudness; recording gain can affect it.
RMS bars show linear amplitude (the values are in dBFS). Bars share a scale within each metric for the selected sentence. Larger is not necessarily better. Rate, pause and formant claims are omitted pending annotation and measurement validation.
Corpus, evidence and next step

The deposit describes one male RP English speaker, 25 sentences, five conditions and two recording microphones; the downloaded WAV archive contains 125 MKH800 files. Here the microphone, speaker and prompt are held fixed. Conditions are elicitation instructions, not perceptual outcomes. Hand-corrected phonetic TextGrids are supplied; they are not listener recognition labels.

Mayo (2013), corpus, DOI 10.7488/ds/138. The only listed licence is a Depositor Agreement; raw audio stays private and local.

Gutz, Maffei & Green (2023) found overlap in Clear and ASR-elicited acoustic changes, including reduced speaking rate and increased vowel distinctiveness. Their ASR accuracy, intelligibility and clarity related to different features. Our acted computer-directed condition does not reproduce their ASR-feedback intervention or listener experiment.

Next: recruit listeners for controlled word transcription in a defined listening condition, then test whether acoustic measures predict held-out listener outcomes across speakers. One speaker cannot establish generalization.
