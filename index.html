1. Project context and problem

The shared context is paediatric VR training: using evidence from a simulated team interaction to support performance assessment and post-simulation debriefing. This project explores both what the assessment can recover from recorded speech and how its findings can help trainees and instructors reflect on performance.

Plain transcripts preserve words, potentially including fillers, but not the original pitch, intensity or detailed timing. Experiments on vocal confidence show that these prosodic cues contribute to listeners’ impressions alongside linguistic content (Jiang & Pell, 2017). This motivates testing whether retaining audio evidence improves assessment beyond transcript/context alone; benefits for paediatric debriefing remain to be evaluated.

The intermediate outputs suggest contextual transcript correction, interpretation of conversational relationships, and summarisation against assessment criteria. They do not establish the exact implementation.

3. Related work and evidence boundaries

Delivery cues and their limits. Response delays, filled pauses and intonation influenced listeners’ estimates of a speaker’s knowledge, with effects depending on whether a response was an answer or a nonanswer (Brennan & Williams, 1995). In a controlled expressive-speech study, unconfident expressions had slower speaking rates and more pauses; pitch and amplitude/intensity patterns also differentiated confidence levels (Jiang & Pell, 2017). These findings motivate candidate features, not universal thresholds or a clinical competence score.

Measurement versus inference. A pause duration is a measurement; hesitation is an interpretation; its cause is a further claim. Perceived confidence (listener ratings), self-reported certainty (speaker ratings), and correctness (task scoring) are distinct targets: Pon-Barry and Shieber annotated all three and found that perceived and self-reported certainty often differed (2010). We will keep these labels separate and check automated measurements against human annotations.

Explicit acoustic evidence. openSMILE is a signal-analysis and feature-extraction toolkit (Eyben et al., 2010); eGeMAPS is a compact, standardised acoustic feature set covering frequency, energy and spectral descriptors (Eyben et al., 2016). These provide interpretable numerical acoustic measurements, not semantic conclusions about confidence or teamwork. Connecting measurements to an assessment rubric requires a separate, validated interpretation stage.

Assessment precedent and data. Naim et al. combined language, prosody and facial features to predict observer ratings in 138 mock interviews from 69 participants (2015). This offers a performance-rating and annotation precedent, but neither its dataset nor its results establish validity for paediatric teamwork.

Practical design precedent. The NUS-hosted VMock guide separates speech delivery, content strength and non-verbal feedback, with replay and time-aligned feedback (VMock, n.d.). It motivates separating delivery from content in our interface; product documentation does not establish the validity of proprietary acoustic scores or reveal the deployed algorithm.

Internal aviation related work. From Roleplay to Virtual Reality provides an adaptive cabin-crew training example (supplied manuscript, n.d.); Signals of Aggression examines multimodal cues and perceived aggression in virtual agents (Ong et al., 2026). Both inform internal discussion of training and perception, not validation of paediatric assessment. The supplied files are not reproduced here.

PVC Definition v0.1

Perceived Vocal Confidence (PVC) is the degree to which a listener judges, from a speaker's vocal delivery, that the speaker sounds certain and committed to what they are saying.

PVC is a listener-perceived communication construct, not a direct measurement of internal confidence, correctness, competence/expertise, anxiety/stress, personality or overall communication quality. Perceived and self-reported certainty can differ (Pon-Barry & Shieber, 2010). This working definition is explicitly Version 0.1, to be refined through literature review, experiments and consultation with a speech expert.

Operational ratings: 1 very low / strongly tentative or doubtful; 2 low; 3 neutral or unclear; 4 high; 5 very high / strongly assured and committed. Ground truth will be an agreed aggregate of multiple independent listeners, retaining disagreement and uncertainty. Individual ratings are ordinal; aggregate regression treats them as approximately continuous.

x = measurable acoustic/temporal features; y = aggregated human PVC rating; PVC_hat = f(x). Learn the mapping to human ratings. Acoustic features predict the construct; they do not define it. Temporal, pitch and intensity cues are motivated by Jiang & Pell (2017); interacting filler, rate and F0 effects by Kirkland et al. (2022). Every feature's sign and importance must be learned and validated: no universal “high pitch = low confidence” rule.

5. Proposed methodology — narrowed PVC study

The paediatric VR assessment/debriefing context remains unchanged. Charlie's immediate target is PVC at an agreed single-speaker analysis unit; this does not replace the existing teamwork rubric or assess clinical correctness.

  1. Human reference. Multiple independent listeners rate identical clips using the PVC definition and 1–5 anchors, blind to model outputs. Agree aggregation, exclusions and inter-rater reliability before the main study. Resolve “neutral” versus “unclear”; use a separate unrateable flag for inaudible clips. Where an ICC is used, justify its form and confidence interval (Koo & Li, 2016).
  2. Engineered pathway. Audio → openSMILE/eGeMAPSv02 plus transparent temporal features → mean baseline, Ridge regression and Random Forest → predicted PVC. Preserve all 88 functionals (Eyben et al., 2010; Eyben et al., 2016). Fit preprocessing inside folds, use speaker-grouped evaluation, and report MAE, Spearman, linear coefficients and forest importance. No arbitrary score weights or deep network.
  3. Audio-LLM benchmark. Same audio → an audio-capable language model using the identical definition and anchors → estimated PVC plus evidence and uncertainty. The separate template uses gpt-audio-1.5, verified against official documentation; it has not been run. SALMONN provides broader audio-language architecture context (Tang et al., 2024), not PVC validation. LLM estimates are not ground truth.

Compare both predictions with independent human ratings on held-out speakers; session/scenario separation, rater agreement, preprocessing sensitivity and measurement checks require an agreed validation protocol. Any later transcript/context ablation should follow confirmation of the existing pipeline. Natural speech still carries lexical meaning, and listener perceptions may depend on accent, speaker and recording conditions.

Kang Zhe's data-presentation workstream remains focused on organising findings, evidence and uncertainty for debriefing. Better PVC prediction would not itself demonstrate improved teamwork or learning; feedback usefulness requires its own evaluation.

6. Implementation and evaluation status

  • Completed work: PVC Definition v0.1, referenced cue families and independent-human-rating formulation; inspection of supplied Before/After WAV and transcript/timestamp materials.
  • Prototype work: executable Python extraction of 88 eGeMAPSv02 functionals plus energy-based temporal measurements, JSON/CSV exports, and a supervised training/prediction scaffold. Real Before Team Leader audio (513.344 s) produced 35.6% energy activity and 188 internal low-energy gaps ≥0.2 s at −35 dBFS. These are measurement outputs, not PVC scores; whole-session gaps may include time when others speak. Missing transcripts/response context remain unavailable.
  • Proposed validation: manually verify segmentation and timing; collect multiple independent listener ratings; quantify rater agreement; evaluate against the mean baseline and audio LLM on held-out speakers. No real labelled PVC model or comparative result is claimed.
  • Separate external benchmark completed: 600 children's reading recordings with published Low/Medium/High labels were processed with our extractor. In five-fold recording-level cross-validation, balanced accuracy was 0.333 for the majority baseline, 0.716 for logistic regression and 0.781 for Random Forest. Speaker IDs could not be verified, so these are not speaker-independent results or adult PVC validation. The provenance, methods and limitations distinguish this benchmark from the final study; a local microphone/WAV demo displays its three-class probabilities with a domain-shift warning.
  • Confound audit: on exactly the same saved folds, eGeMAPS-only balanced accuracy was 0.733 (logistic) and 0.769 (Forest), while duration alone reached 0.569 and 0.539. The added duration/RMS fields do not explain the full result; residual speaker/task/recording confounds remain. No model was selected or replaced after this audit. See the ablation and final meeting status.
  • Unresolved questions: analysis unit, listener panel, neutral/unclear anchor, data permissions, role-to-speaker identity, threshold sensitivity, recording/overlap effects and integration with debriefing.

Prototype, runnable commands and demonstration record. The After file measured only 0.03% energy activity at the same gate, showing strong recording-level/threshold sensitivity rather than a PVC difference. Existing cleaned transcripts contain changed wording and cannot be assumed to preserve fillers. The supplied teamwork assessments must not be relabelled as PVC ground truth.
