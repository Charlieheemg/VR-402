# Interim presentation briefing and conversation summary

## The one-minute narrative

Our project starts from an existing VR paediatric emergency simulation and automated communication-assessment pipeline. That prior work already reconstructs multi-speaker discourse, applies a clinically refined seven-category rubric and produces evidence-linked Excel feedback. Our FYP therefore focuses on a different problem: **how assessed information is converted into useful post-simulation learning and debriefing feedback**. We selected Hattie & Timperley (2007) to structure goals, current performance and next actions, and Nicol & Macfarlane-Dick (2006) to support learner self-assessment and instructors' use of feedback. We have conceptual learner and instructor formats and have inventoried consultant annotations. We have not yet validated educational benefit or prioritisation rules; these are the next research questions.

## How the direction evolved

1. Initial project understanding: VR, transcription, Excel assessment and AI feedback already existed; building a new VR simulator or classifier was not the main opportunity.
2. Preliminary issue: current output is transcript-heavy; users must identify relevant observations and infer next learning actions. This is a *design hypothesis* rather than proven learner dissatisfaction.
3. Personalisation explored through self-relevance and self-prioritisation; rejected as the main pedagogy because it did not adequately specify an educational feedback process. Citation count alone is not a validity test.
4. Feedback timing reviewed: real-time cues can improve some objective procedural tasks (e.g., CPR quality), but interruption may interfere with complex team learning. Post-simulation feedback was selected as a scoped design decision, not a claim of universal superiority.
5. Educational theories chosen: Hattie & Timperley 2007 (feed up/back/forward; task/process/self-regulation/self) and Nicol & Macfarlane-Dick 2006 (seven formative-feedback principles).
6. Existing manuscript supplied seven operationalised categories, evidence-linked assessment and preliminary behavioural outcome measurement. Researcher confirmed CT and NN consultant evaluations are expert reference material.
7. Consultant workbooks inventoried; apparent annotation-density differences motivate preserving spans, not equating comment counts with competency or severity.
8. Proposed redesign: learner report for own role and next action; instructor report for team overview and debrief discussion. Both derived from the same evidence, with no unvalidated ranking.
9. Prioritisation separated from restructuring: assessment validity gate -> audience routing -> *future* emphasis rules. Five factors previously discussed were heuristics, **not** a validated theory or weighted algorithm.

## Examiner questions to anticipate

**Why not just upload Excel to ChatGPT?** It can produce summaries. The intended research contribution is an explicit, auditable, theoretically derived transformation plus empirical comparison against a generic LLM summary. Superiority has not been demonstrated.

**Is this a new AI assessment model?** No. It builds on the existing expert-aligned seven-category assessment. Avoid claiming ownership of the existing pipeline or manuscript results.

**Why post-feedback?** Scoping choice for complex team behaviours and reflective debriefing; real-time feedback has benefits for other targets. No universal timing superiority is claimed.

**Why these two pedagogies?** Complementary: feedback information structure and learner/teacher use. Highly influential, but citations alone do not prove effectiveness of our interface.

**Why two views?** Learners need performance-gap recognition and next actions; instructors need team patterns and discussion prompts. This is a theoretically motivated hypothesis, not yet validated.

**How are categories prioritised?** They are not yet ranked. Expert severity judgments and educational priority criteria remain open. Evidence validity and audience routing are not the same as priority.

**How do you know it works?** We do not yet. Initial work is conceptual/data feasibility; planned testing measures factual fidelity, comprehension, action plans and subsequent behaviour.
