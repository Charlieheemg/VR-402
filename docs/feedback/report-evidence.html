1. Worked feedback examples

These concepts adapt the author-supplied Word examples document. Worksheet locations below reproduce its source pointers; the original workbook and transcript have not been independently checked. Observations are paraphrased from that document. Behavioural targets and practice suggestions are proposed design content requiring expert review.

Example 1 · Learner view · Team leader

Make task ownership explicit

Clear Messages / Roles & Responsibilities · Summary!B100/F100 and B102/D102

Target · feed up
Make the intended recipient and task clear, using the approved communication rubric.
Observation · feed back
The supplied example describes an undirected CPR request at F100 and an unclear bagging assignment requiring correction at D102. These are worksheet interpretations, not independently verified transcript findings.
Reflection
How did you establish who owned each task? What evidence showed that the intended receiver understood?
Next practice · feed forward
Practise naming the intended recipient, stating the task and seeking acknowledgement. The educator should confirm the wording and applicability to the scenario.
Interpretation boundary
An unclear exchange does not establish clinical harm or general communication incompetence. Preserve the surrounding dialogue and any subsequent repair.

Example 2 · Learner view · IV role

Build on clarification, then apply it consistently

Knowing One’s Limitations / Constructive Intervention · Summary!I108 and H169

Target · feed up
Make uncertainty visible and request clarification when responsibilities or instructions are incomplete, subject to the approved rubric.
Observation · feed back
The document describes a positive clarification request about bagging at I108. At H169, it describes acknowledgement of a medication instruction without clarification of a missing route. Both observations should remain visible so feedback retains a strength as well as a potential gap.
Reflection
What prompted you to clarify the role assignment? What would prompt clarification when an instruction is incomplete?
Next practice · feed forward
Retain the clarification behaviour and rehearse requesting missing information in a later scenario, with clinical requirements determined by the educator.
Interpretation boundary
This example does not establish that medication was administered incorrectly. Confirm the complete exchange and the intended recipient before attributing a failure.

3. Evaluation protocol

Stage 1 · Does the transformation preserve evidence?

Select 2–3 permission-cleared episodes covering positive and negative evidence and at least two competencies. Freeze the source spans and rubric version. Produce a generic summary using a documented prompt and a theory-guided version from identical evidence; retain the worksheet excerpt as the baseline. Record model/version and generation settings if an LLM is used.

A reviewer other than the drafter should check each factual claim. Log example ID, source pointer, claim, check result, reviewer and revision. Use pass, fail or unresolved; an unavailable source is unresolved, not a pass.

  • Provenance: can the cited source span be located?
  • Meaning: are category, polarity and contextual qualifications retained?
  • Recipient: is individual, role or team attribution supported?
  • Completeness: have relevant positive evidence or contextual repairs been omitted?
  • Added content: are suggestions separated from observations, without invented scores, severity or outcomes?

Report passed, failed and unresolved checks with their denominators, plus concrete corrections. All factual claims in a demonstrator must be resolved before it is presented as verified. This is a development gate, not a validated clinical threshold. Current status: not performed.

Stage 2 · Does the structure improve feedback use?

After expert review and relevant approvals, pilot a generic-summary versus theory-guided comparison. Include the worksheet condition only if recruitment and study burden permit. Counterbalance matched cases so exposure to one version does not reveal another version of the same episode. Keep the underlying assessment constant.

  • Learners: source-supported gap identification, expert-rated corrective-plan quality, review time and perceived relevance.
  • Instructors: accuracy and time required to prepare a source-supported debrief plan, plus perceived usefulness.
  • Analysis: prespecify scoring anchors; blind outcome raters where feasible; record disagreement; account for repeated participants and cases. Use pilot variance and feasibility to justify sample size.

Subsequent behaviour is a later outcome. If the prior study’s balance rate B = (G − R)/(G + R) is adopted, define independent behaviour instances and comparable observation opportunities; B is undefined when G + R = 0. Team dependence, annotation density and baseline differences must be addressed. Immediate comprehension does not establish retention or clinical transfer.
