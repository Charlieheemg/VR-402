B1. From a generic prompt to a specific learning task

The original concept document drew on worksheet material whose redistribution permission is unconfirmed. Its source-derived quotations and pointers are withheld in this revision. The design principles remain: make the criterion visible, connect it to evidence, ask the learner to interpret the gap, and support a concrete practice goal.

Synthetic learner example

For the invented request “Could someone prepare the equipment?”, ask the learner to identify the intended receiver and action. A revised request should supply what is missing under the existing Clear Messages definition. This is an illustration, not an observed clinical exchange.

Attribution and context

If surrounding turns already identify the receiver, the isolated sentence may understate the available information. The full exchange must be checked before calling the request incomplete or assigning responsibility.

Synthetic instructor example

Ask team members to explain how they interpreted the same request, compare their answers, and agree a specific practice goal. An educator determines the clinical detail. These examples have not been evaluated with learners.

B3. Proposed educational evaluation

The educational frameworks guide feedback content. Evaluation requires evidence that supports its interpretation and use. Cook and Hatala argue that validity concerns the intended interpretation and decision in a particular context. [23] Carless and Boud distinguish understanding feedback from taking action on it. [22] Accordingly, evaluation separates feedback fidelity and quality, immediate educational usefulness, and subsequent learning effectiveness. This is a project-specific synthesis, not a validated three-level instrument.

Research questions and operational definitions

RQ-B1 — Feedback fidelity and quality: Can learner- and instructor-facing feedback preserve assessment evidence, meaning, category/polarity and defensible individual/team attribution? How clear, relevant and actionable is the output when assessed separately?

RQ-B2 — Immediate educational usefulness: Compared with the original worksheet using identical assessment evidence, does theory-guided feedback improve learner comprehension, corrective learning-plan quality and instructors’ preparation of accurate, actionable debriefs?

RQ-B3 — Subsequent learning effectiveness: Does theory-guided feedback improve communication performance in subsequent simulation scenarios?

Appendix C provides partial evidence for RQ-B1 through mechanical checks. Semantic fidelity and feedback quality remain unestablished. RQ-B2 and RQ-B3 remain unanswered. The original assessments and expert annotations belong to the upstream research team.

Comprehension concerns interpretation of expected and observed behaviour, including performance-gap recognition and evidence limitations. Corrective learning-plan quality concerns a feasible, specific and appropriate proposed response. Debrief preparation quality concerns accurate, evidence-supported discussion themes and actionable facilitation plans. Each outcome requires prespecified scoring criteria and rater guidance. None is an established score for this interface.

B3.1 Feedback fidelity and quality

Fidelity is a prerequisite: reviewers will check source traceability, rubric category/polarity, contextual meaning, supported speaker/role/team attribution, omitted repairs and unsupported additions. Clarity, recipient relevance and actionability will be assessed separately, without combining them with fidelity into an unvalidated total score. Clinical experts may review appropriateness; target users may judge comprehensibility.

The Feedback Quality Instrument provides candidate review domains but was developed for educator–learner conversations, not static AI reports. [24] A 2024 scoping review identified limited evidence for digital feedback assessment tools. [26] Any adapted items therefore require local content review and piloting. The upstream team’s expert annotations provide assessment evidence, not validation of this presentation.

B3.2 Immediate educational usefulness

Learner outcomes are comprehension of expected and observed behaviour—including recognition of performance gaps—and the quality of corrective learning plans. Instructor outcomes are the accuracy, usefulness and quality of debrief preparation; review/preparation time is secondary. A stated intention or plan does not demonstrate actual feedback uptake, which requires observation of subsequent use. [22]

The proposed primary comparison uses the original worksheet and theory-guided feedback from the same fixed assessment evidence. A generic summary is a secondary comparator if feasible; an external LLM is not required. This baseline was reconciled with the main report on 11 November 2026 so the study tests improvement over the existing workflow. Future protocol elements include counterbalanced cases, documented generation procedures, prespecified scoring anchors, blinded outcome ratings where feasible and a pilot-based sample-size justification. Learner and instructor outcomes will be analysed separately, accounting for repeated participants, cases and teams.

B3.3 Subsequent learning effectiveness

Later communication performance will, where feasible, be independently assessed in comparable simulations using the existing seven competencies. Kirkpatrick distinguishes reactions, learning, workplace behaviour and downstream results; its use in healthcare simulation does not make these outcomes interchangeable. [25] [27] Improved comprehension or subsequent VR performance alone cannot establish retention, workplace transfer or patient benefit.

The upstream manuscript’s balance rate, B = (G − R)/(G + R), is a candidate metric only. It is undefined when G + R = 0 and depends on event definitions, annotation practices, team dependence and comparable opportunities to demonstrate behaviour. These assumptions require review before the metric is adopted.

B3.4 Feasibility and pilot procedures

Completed engineering tests and current limitations are maintained in Appendix C; aggregate inspection of the supplied report is in Appendix D. The following study is planned. It depends on source permissions, verification, expert review and relevant approvals.

Select 2–3 permitted episodes across at least two competencies, including positive and negative observations. Freeze source spans and rubric versions; retain original evidence and worksheets. Generate comparison outputs from identical inputs using documented prompts, model/version and settings where applicable.

An independent reviewer should log each claim, source pointer, category/polarity, attribution, omitted repairs, unsupported additions, decision and revision. Record pass, fail or unresolved with denominators; unavailable evidence remains unresolved. Separately review clarity, relevance and actionability with clinical experts and intended users as appropriate. Do not average these judgements with fidelity checks.

In a later approved pilot, counterbalance matched cases to avoid repeated exposure to versions of the same episode, prespecify learner and instructor scoring criteria, blind outcome raters where feasible and document disagreement. Use pilot feasibility and variance to justify sample size and repeated-measure analysis. Perceived usefulness and preparation time complement, rather than replace, source-supported task performance. Observe subsequent use before claiming feedback uptake.
