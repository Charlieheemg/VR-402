# Expert annotation evidence: inventory and interpretation limits

**Provenance:** research-team consultant annotations described in the supplied project package; original workbooks are not included here. This document records what the supplied package *reports*, not a fresh independent re-analysis of private files.

## Reported inventory

The earlier working package states that **20 expert-annotation workbooks across 10 paired session identifiers (CT and NN)** were inventoried. It reports a preliminary extraction of **268 populated CT cells** and **63 populated NN cells**. These are counts of populated annotation **cells**, not independent behavioural episodes, directly comparable assessment rates, validated inter-rater agreement, or measures of educational priority. The source package does not provide the underlying workbook rows here, so these counts have **not been independently recomputed in this documentation update**.

## What this does and does not establish

- **Supports:** an initial assessment that source annotations may be available for developing traceable feedback examples, subject to permissions and schema inspection.
- **Does not support:** claims about prevalence of behaviour, comparative performance of CT versus NN, clinical severity, feedback usefulness, inter-rater reliability or effectiveness of a redesigned interface.
- **Key threats:** differences in annotation density, span boundaries, event definitions, speaker attribution and whether one episode occupies multiple cells.

## Proposed next audit (not completed)

1. Confirm provenance, workbook versions, permission for derived examples and de-identification rules.
2. Define the unit of analysis (cell, span, event, speaker, team and session); preserve all denominators.
3. Reconcile each candidate feedback statement to the source workbook and original transcript span.
4. Record ambiguous, missing and overlapping labels; do not infer agreement from counts alone.
5. Use only permission-cleared, de-identified aggregate findings or synthetic examples in this public repository.

The original project manuscript's expert-comparison results belong to the **existing research team** [P1]. They are not a validation of the proposed learner/instructor feedback redesign.
