E1. How the project developed

This is a synthesis of the work documented in the repository, not a retrospective claim that every experiment followed the current hypothesis. The initial work explored perceived vocal confidence, acoustic measurement and feedback concepts. Subsequent tests exposed measurement, transfer and fidelity problems. Examiner feedback narrowed the speech contribution to evidence within the existing seven categories. The resulting plan treats independent interpretation and educational evaluation as necessary next steps.

E4. Proposed studies and decision gates

No participant numbers, approvals or effects are asserted here. The eight-week sequence in the main report begins after scope and access confirmation. Pilot burden, usable independent units and scoring reliability determine whether a subsequent comparison is feasible.

Deliverable at each gate: Weeks 1–2, reviewed definitions and access decision; Weeks 3–4, auditable episode inventory and corrected report outputs with regression evidence; Weeks 5–6, pilot disagreement/burden log and sample-size rationale; Weeks 7–8, a justified comparative analysis or an explicit decision to revise/defer it. Reviewer availability and recruitment are dependencies, not commitments already secured.

For an examiner, a negative finding can still resolve the research question: exclude an unreliable audio cue or reject a feedback format that distorts evidence. The intended deliverables are defensible findings and inspectable methods; clinical deployment is outside the demonstrated interim scope.
